This economic assessment focuses on evaluating the investment and operational costs associated with the proposed integrated energy system. The analysis encompasses the estimation of capital expenditures (CAPEX) and operational expenditures (OPEX) for each subsystem, followed by the calculation of the expected revenues generated by the overall plant. Subsequently, the Net Present Value (NPV) of the investment is determined to assess the economic viability of the solution. Finally, a sensitivity analysis is performed to investigate the impact of key techno-economic parameters on the profitability and robustness of the proposed configuration. 

Moreover, the analysis has been conducted from the perspective of a public-sector energy agency, operating as a government-backed institution responsible for implementing decarbonization strategies in the designated geographical region. This agency, due to the hybrid nature of energy generation, is assumed to receive dedicated funding from the Italian government, specifically in the form of zero-interest loans aimed at supporting the deployment of the proposed low-carbon energy infrastructure. Such financial conditions significantly influence the economic assessment, as they reduce the cost of capital and enhance the financial viability of the project.

\subsection{CAPEX}
The estimation of capital expenditures (CAPEX) has been carried out for the main components of the proposed energy infrastructure, namely the E-SMRs, high-temperature steam electrolysers (HTSE), the hydrogen storage buffer, the hydrogen compressor, the gas turbine, and the methanation reactor. It is important to note that the balance of plant (BoP) costs and system integration costs have already been incorporated within the CAPEX figures reported for each of these major subsystems. Consequently, BoP and integration do not constitute separate cost items in this analysis, ensuring that the CAPEX values entirely reflect the overall investment required to deploy each unit as an operational part of the integrated plant.

\subsubsection {Capex for E-SMRs}
The capital expenditures (CAPEX) for the electrochemical Small Modular Reactors (e-SMRs) have been estimated based on data from the existing literature related to the TANDEM project. According to this literature, both reactors are assumed to have a technical lifetime of 60 years, and the specific investment cost, referred to the year 2023, is estimated at 6,050,000 EUR per MWe. This results in a total CAPEX of approximately 2,141.7 million EUR for the e-SMRs, considering a combined electrical output capacity of 354 MWe. This cost estimation is directly linked to the design choices adopted in our system modeling, as the components implemented in our Modelica simulations were selected from the same libraries used within the TANDEM project framework, ensuring consistency between technical modeling and economic assessment.

It is worth noting that the CAPEX value of 6,050,000 EUR/MWe originates from a literature review conducted by CEA within the TANDEM project, where different references report investment costs ranging from 3,874,000 EUR/MWe [4] to 7,712,000 EUR/MWe [5]. By averaging across multiple sources, CEA proposed adopting a reference value of 6,050,000 EUR/MWe for economic assessments (TANDEM Project Documentation, 2023) (Lazard. (2023). Lazard’s Levelized Cost of Energy Analysis—Version 16.0., NEA. (2022). The Role of Nuclear Power in the Hydrogen Economy: Cost and Competitiveness. NEA No. 7630, OECD.) Furthermore, similar investment cost levels are reported in other studies, such as the work by Jussi-Pekka Ikonen, Tomi J. Lindroos, and Pauli Hiltunen, which estimates the investment cost of SMR systems at approximately 2,057 million EUR, excluding integration and balance of plant costs (Ikonen et al., “Feasibility of small modular reactors for decarbonizing district heating systems: a case study of the Helsinki metropolitan area,” 2022). These values support the validity of the cost assumptions adopted in our analysis.

\subsubsection {Capex for HTSE}
The CAPEX for the high-temperature steam electrolysers (HTSE) have been estimated again using data directly derived from the literature of the TANDEM project, in line with the methodology adopted for the e-SMRs. According to the TANDEM documentation, the specific investment cost for HTSE technology is approximately 780,000 EUR per MW of nominal power. Given that our system design includes fifteen electrolyser units, each with a nominal power capacity of 21 MW, this leads to a total CAPEX of approximately 245.7 million EUR for the HTSE section. 

Each HTSE unit is assumed to have a technical lifetime of around 20 years. Since our economic analysis considers a 60-year project horizon, the HTSE systems will need to be replaced twice after the initial investment. It is, however, reasonable to assume that the cost of HTSE technology will decrease over time as the technology matures and economies of scale are realized. To estimate the future reduction in CAPEX for HTSE systems, we relied on cost trends observed in the hydrogen market. The report 'The European Hydrogen Market Landscape' (2024) highlights that the production cost of green hydrogen decreased by 0.25 EUR/kg over one year, falling from 6.86 EUR/kg to 6.61 EUR/kg, corresponding to an annual reduction of approximately 3.5%. Conversely, data from the German market indicate a period of price stability between March and June 2025 (European Energy Exchange, 2025). Balancing these observations, we have adopted an average annual cost reduction of 1.75% to project future CAPEX declines. Applying this annual reduction, we estimate that CAPEX for HTSE units will decrease by approximately 30% after the first 20-year period. In the subsequent 20-year period, it is anticipated that technological improvements will continue, albeit at a slower pace as the technology reaches maturity. Therefore, for the second replacement cycle, a more conservative annual decrease rate of 0.875% has been assumed, corresponding to a cumulative CAPEX reduction of 15% over the second 20-year period. This anticipated decrease is primarily attributed to technological learning curves and increased manufacturing efficiencies as HTSE technology becomes more established in the market.

\subsubsection {Capex for Hydrogen Storage Buffers}
Also the CAPEX for the hydrogen buffer storage system have been estimated looking at the TANDEM project, consistent with the methodology applied for the other subsystems. According to the TANDEM documentation, the specific investment cost for hydrogen storage is reported as 490 EUR per kg of hydrogen storage capacity. 

In our system design, the hydrogen buffer has been sized to accommodate a maximum of one week of hydrogen production at full nominal capacity. Specifically, the storage capacity has been calculated considering a nominal production flow of 0.1 kg/s per electrolyser module, with fifteen modules operating simultaneously, resulting in a weekly storage requirement of approximately 100 tonnes:

Storage capacity = 0.1 kg/s × 15 modules × 3600 s/h × 24 h/day × 0.1 × 7 days ≈ 100,000 kg.

Based on this storage capacity, the total CAPEX for the hydrogen buffer is estimated at approximately 49 million EUR. Similar to the assumptions made for the HTSE units, the hydrogen buffer is assumed to have a technical lifetime of 20 years, implying that it will need to be replaced twice during the 60-year project horizon. Moreover, the same cost reduction assumptions adopted for HTSE technology are applied to the hydrogen storage system.
