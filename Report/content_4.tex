\section{Economic Study}

\subsection{Introduction}
This economic assessment focuses on evaluating the investment and operational costs associated with the proposed integrated energy system. The analysis encompasses the estimation of capital expenditures (CAPEX) and operational expenditures (OPEX) for each subsystem, followed by the calculation of the expected revenues generated by the overall plant. Subsequently, the Net Present Value (NPV) of the investment is determined to assess the economic viability of the solution. Finally, a sensitivity analysis is performed to investigate the impact of key techno-economic parameters on the profitability and robustness of the proposed configuration. 

Moreover, the analysis has been conducted from the perspective of a public-sector energy agency, operating as a government-backed institution responsible for implementing decarbonization strategies in the designated geographical region. This agency, due to the hybrid nature of energy generation, is assumed to receive dedicated funding from the Italian government, specifically in the form of zero-interest loans aimed at supporting the deployment of the proposed low-carbon energy infrastructure. 

It's also important to underline that the assessment has been conducted over a total project lifespan of 60 years, divided into four incremental scenarios of 20 years each as previously described. The starting year of the analysis has been set to 2025, which is acknowledged as not practically feasible for the commissioning of such a complex infrastructure. However, this assumption has been adopted due to the lack of sufficiently reliable long-term forecasts in the current literature, allowing for a consistent comparative framework. The initial scenario considers the integrated system to be fully operational from the outset, although preliminary calculations indicate that such a configuration would not be economically sustainable under current market conditions. Consequently, a second scenario has been investigated in which the plant initially operates as a fully electric solution, with future integration of hydrogen and syngas production capacities.

\subsection{CAPEX}
The estimation of capital expenditures (CAPEX) has been carried out for the main components of the proposed energy infrastructure, namely the E-SMRs, high-temperature steam electrolysers (HTSE), the hydrogen storage buffer, the hydrogen compressor, the gas turbine, and the methanation reactor. It is important to note that the balance of plant (BoP) costs and system integration costs have already been incorporated within the CAPEX figures reported for each of these major subsystems. Consequently, BoP and integration do not constitute separate cost items in this analysis, ensuring that the CAPEX values entirely reflect the overall investment required to deploy each unit as an operational part of the integrated plant.

\subsubsection {Capex for E-SMRs}
The capital expenditures (CAPEX) for the E-SMRs have been estimated based on existing literature related to the TANDEM project. According to it, both reactors are assumed to have a technical lifetime of 60 years, and the specific investment cost, referred to the year 2023, is estimated at 6,050,000 EUR per MWe. This value emerges from the review conducted by CEA, where different references report investment costs ranging from 3,874,000 EUR/MWe to 7,712,000 EUR/MWe (TANDEM Project Documentation, 2023, Lazard. (2023). Lazard’s Levelized Cost of Energy Analysis—Version 16.0., NEA. (2022) The Role of Nuclear Power in the Hydrogen Economy: Cost and Competitiveness. NEA No. 7630, OECD.) This results in a total CAPEX of approximately 2,141.7 million EUR, considering a combined electrical output capacity of 354 MWe and excluding integration and BoP costs. This cost estimation is directly linked to the design choices adopted in the Modelica simulations, where the same libraries used within the TANDEM project framework have been selected, ensuring consistency between technical modeling and economic assessment.

\subsubsection {Capex for HTSE}
The CAPEX for the HTSEs have been estimated again from the literature of the TANDEM project, in line with the methodology adopted for the E-SMRs. According to the TANDEM documentation, the specific investment cost for HTSE technology is approximately 780,000 EUR per MW of nominal power. Given that our system design includes fifteen electrolyser units, each with a nominal power capacity of 21 MW, this leads to a total CAPEX of approximately 245.7 million EUR for the HTSE section. 

Each HTSE unit is assumed to have a technical lifetime of around 20 years, so that considering a 60-year project horizon, the units will need to be replaced twice after the initial investment. It is, however, reasonable to assume that the cost of HTSE technology will decrease over time as the technology matures and economies of scale are realized. The estimation of the future reduction in CAPEX relies on the cost trends observed in the hydrogen market. The report 'The European Hydrogen Market Landscape' (2024) highlights that the production cost of green hydrogen decreased by 0.25 EUR/kg over one year, falling from 6.86 EUR/kg to 6.61 EUR/kg, corresponding to an annual reduction of approximately 3.5%. Conversely, data from the German market indicate a period of price stability between March and June 2025 (European Energy Exchange, 2025). Balancing these observations, an average annual cost reduction of 1.75% is plausible, leading to a decrease of approximately 30% after the first 20-year period. In the subsequent 20-year period, technological improvements will continue, albeit at a slower pace as the technology reaches maturity. Therefore, for the second replacement cycle, a more conservative annual decrease rate of 0.875% has been assumed, corresponding to a cumulative CAPEX reduction of 15% over the second 20-year period. This anticipated decrease is primarily attributed to technological learning curves and increased manufacturing efficiencies as HTSE technology becomes more established in the market.

\subsubsection {Capex for Hydrogen Storage Buffer}
Also the CAPEX for the hydrogen buffer storage system have been estimated looking at the TANDEM project, consistently with the methodology applied for the other subsystems. In this case the specific investment cost for hydrogen storage is reported as 490 EUR per kg of hydrogen storage capacity. The hydrogen buffer has been sized to accommodate a maximum of one week of hydrogen production at full nominal capacity. Specifically, the storage capacity has been calculated considering a nominal production flow of 0.1 kg/s per electrolyser module, with fifteen modules operating simultaneously, resulting in a weekly storage requirement of approximately 100 tonnes:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Storage\ capacity\ [kg]} = 0.1 \times 15 \times 3600 \times 24 \times 0.1 \times 7 \approx 100{,}000
\end{equation}

Based on this storage capacity, the total CAPEX for the hydrogen buffer is estimated at approximately 49 million EUR (TANDEM Project Documentation). Similar to the assumptions made for the HTSE units, the hydrogen buffer is assumed to have a technical lifetime of 20 years, implying that it will need to be replaced twice during the 60-year project horizon, and the same cost reduction assumptions adopted for HTSE technology are applied.

\subsubsection {Capex for Hydrogen Compressor}
CAPEX have been still estimated based on data from the TANDEM project. The specific investment cost is reported as 1,000,000 EUR per MW of electrical power input, and the required compressor capacity has been determined using TANDEM’s reference, which indicates that compressing a hydrogen mass flow rate of 8,257 kg/h requires 17.82 MW of electrical power. Adapting this ratio to mass flow rates results in a required compressor capacity of approximately 1.5 MW, leading to a total CAPEX of around 1,500,000 EUR. The compressor is assumed by TANDEM to have a lifetime of 10 years, meaning it must be replaced five times over the 60-year project horizon. The same cost reduction dynamics assumed for HTSE units have been applied.

\subsubsection {Capex for Gas Turbine}
The CAPEX for the gas turbine LM6000 have been estimated based on data reported in the study by Riboldi et al. (2020), which analyzed the environmental and economic impacts of offshore oil platform electrification. According to this study, the investment cost for one LM6000 gas turbine is approximately 23.2 million EUR. Although the system design requires a gas turbine capacity of 47 MW, to adopt a conservative approach, cost figures were retained corresponding to the 57 MW LM6000 unit as reported in Riboldi et al. (2020), given that the model and technology are identical to those implemented in the selected design. Therefore, the CAPEX for the gas turbine has been estimated at 23.2 million EUR. The operational lifetime of the LM6000 gas turbine is assumed to be approximately 20 years, implying that it will need to be replaced twice over the 60-year project horizon.

\subsubsection {Capex for Methanation Reactor}
The CAPEX for the methanation reactor have been estimated at approximately 8.19 million EUR. This value has been derived based on the work of Götz et al. (2016), who reported that the investment cost of a methanation unit is roughly one-thirtieth of the cost of electrolysers. Accordingly, the same ratio was applied to specific electrolyser CAPEX figures to estimate the cost of the methanation subsystem in our project. The methanation reactor is assumed to have a technical lifetime of 20 years, implying that it will need to be replaced twice during the 60-year project horizon. Similar to the HTSE units, the methanation reactor is expected to benefit from cost reductions over time as the technology matures.

\subsection{OPEX}
The estimation of operational expenditures (OPEX) for the proposed energy system has been carried out primarily based on data and assumptions provided in the TANDEM project documentation. This ensures consistency with the methodology used for CAPEX evaluations and aligns the economic assessment with realistic operating conditions and cost structures for each subsystem. The OPEX analysis includes costs related to operation, maintenance, consumables, and other recurring expenses over the plant’s lifetime.

\subsubsection {Opex for E-SMRs}
The operational expenditures (OPEX) for the electrochemical Small Modular Reactors (e-SMRs) have been estimated based primarily on the TANDEM project documentation. According to this source, the variable costs for e-SMR operation—including personnel, operations and maintenance, consumable materials and equipment, replacement costs, and insurance—amount to 24.4 EUR2023 per MWh of electrical output. Additionally, fuel costs are reported as 7.4 EUR2023 per MWh. Therefore, the total operating cost for the e-SMR system is calculated as 31.8 EUR2023/MWhe.

Considering a combined electrical capacity of 354 MW for the two e-SMR units, operating for 8,750 hours per year at a load factor of 0.98, the annual OPEX amounts to approximately 96,640,811 EUR:

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Annual\ OPEX\ [EUR/year]} = 354 \times 8{,}750 \times 0.98 \times 31.8 \approx 96{,}640{,}811
\end{equation}

Moreover, as highlighted in Riboldi et al. (2024), OPEX for e-SMRs are expected to decrease over time as the technology transitions from First-of-a-Kind (FOAK) to Nth-of-a-Kind (NOAK) status. Specifically, after the initial 15 years of operation, operational costs are projected to decrease by approximately 30% due to increased technological maturity and operational experience. During the subsequent 15 years, a further reduction of 10% is anticipated, reflecting continued but slower learning curve effects. Beyond the first 30 years, OPEX levels are expected to stabilize, approaching their asymptotic lower limit. Unlike CAPEX reductions, these OPEX decreases have been modeled as gradual annual reductions distributed evenly over each 15-year period, ensuring that the cumulative reductions align with the projected total decreases of 30% and 10%, respectively.

\subsubsection {Opex for HTSEs}
The OPEX for the HTSE have been estimated based on data from the TANDEM project, which specifies them as an annual 2.5% of the corresponding CAPEX. These costs typically encompass expenses related to routine operations and maintenance, personnel, periodic replacement of components, consumables, and insurance, ensuring the continuous and reliable operation of the electrolyser units, and this corresponds to an annual OPEX of approximately 6.1425 million EUR.

As with the CAPEX projections, the OPEX for HTSE units is expected to decrease over time due to technological learning and efficiency gains. The same cost reduction dynamics assumed for CAPEX, namely a 30% reduction after the first 20 years and an additional 15% reduction in the following 20 years—have been applied to OPEX as well. However, as explained in the previous section, these reductions have been distributed annually over each period to reflect a gradual decrease rather than abrupt step changes, capturing the progressive nature of technological maturation and cost improvements.

\subsubsection {Opex for Hydrogen Storage Buffer}
Tandem in this case indicates annual OPEX equal to 1.5% of the CAPEX. These costs typically cover routine maintenance, safety monitoring, minor repairs, consumables, and insurance to ensure reliable operation, resulting in approximately 735,000 EUR per year. As with the electrolysers, the OPEX for hydrogen storage is expected to decrease over time, following the same cost reduction trends applied to the CAPEX of hydrogen technologies.

\subsubsection {Opex for Hydrogen Compressor}
The TANDEM OPEX for the hydrogen compressor have been estimated equal to 1% of the CAPEX, resulting in 15,000 EUR a year. These costs typically include routine maintenance, periodic inspections, lubrication and cooling system upkeep, minor repairs, and insurance to ensure continuous and safe compressor operation. Similar to the HTSE units, the OPEX for the hydrogen compressor is expected to decrease over time, following the same cost reduction trajectory assumed for hydrogen technologies.

\subsubsection {Opex for Gas Turbine}
The operational expenditures (OPEX) for the LM6000 gas turbine have been estimated using data from Gas Turbine World (2024), which reports fixed O&M costs of 16.30 USD/kW installed. The designed system features a capacity of 47 MW, and this results in a total installed cost of approximately 45.19 million EUR and fixed O&M costs of about 6,965 EUR per MW annually, after conversion from USD and applying an exchange rate of 1.17 USD/EUR. Therefore, the total annual OPEX for the LM6000 gas turbine is approximately 327,355 EUR. Since the project already secures its fuel supply from other subsystems, we have conservatively assumed that fuel-related OPEX can be reduced by 50%, leading to this final estimate.

\subsubsection {Opex for Methanation Reactor}
The OPEX of a Methanation Reactor are significantly influenced by the costs of its input streams, particularly hydrogen and carbon dioxide (CO₂). While hydrogen is produced on-site, the required CO₂ must be sourced externally to enable the production of synthetic methane and to contribute to decarbonization objectives. It has been assumed that CO₂ is captured from industrial processes, where capture costs largely depend on the concentration of CO₂ in the flue gas stream. Higher CO₂ concentrations generally reduce capture costs due to lower energy and solvent consumption during separation.

Two primary references have been considered for estimating CO₂ capture costs. The first, the Global CCS Institute report (2017), indicates costs ranging from 30 to 70 EUR/ton CO₂ depending on the industrial source, from coal-based to natural gas processes. The second study by Smith et al. (2024) reports an average cost of approximately 60 EUR/ton CO₂, although without specifying the industrial source. Based on these data, a mean CO₂ capture cost of 55 EUR/ton CO₂ has been assumed as a balanced compromise between the reported values.

Furthermore, future projections for CO₂ capture costs have been informed by findings from Gruber et al. (2022), who suggest that ongoing research and technological improvements are expected to reduce these costs of approximately 73.5% (from 95 USD/ton to 25 in the long term), leading the initial 55 EUR/ton CO₂ to an estimated cost of roughly 14.5 EUR/ton CO₂ by around 2085. This cost reduction has been implemented as a gradual decrease over time, consistent with anticipated technology learning curves and market developments.

The revenue streams for the proposed integrated system originate from multiple products and market segments. Specifically, revenues are generated through the sale of electricity to the local energy community (Comunità Energetica Rinnovabile, CER) and, when production exceeds local demand, through sales to the wholesale electricity market. Additionally, revenues are derived from hydrogen sales targeted primarily at the transport sector, as well as from synthetic gas (syngas) sales intended to meet the residual fossil fuel demand within the region and, in case of surplus production, to external markets beyond the project’s geographical scope.

\subsubsection {Electricity Revenues}
For electricity, revenues have been computed as the sum of earnings from sales to the CER and revenues from surplus electricity dispatched to the wholesale market. Revenues from sales to the CER have been determined by considering the total annual electricity output delivered to the community, as calculated by the Modelica simulation outputs. The electricity price applied for these calculations has been derived from historical data of the zonal day-ahead electricity price (PUN zonale) for the Northern Italian market area, adjusted by a discount factor to promote public acceptance of the infrastructure.

Specifically, the reference electricity price has been established by analyzing the monthly PUN zonale data recorded from 2022 to the present. An initial estimation of the zonal price for the remaining months of 2025 was obtained by extrapolating the trend observed in 2023 and 2024 through linear interpolation. Subsequently, a weighted average monthly price over the past three years was calculated, assigning higher weights to more recent data to reflect current market dynamics. Finally, the average annual price was determined by computing a weighted mean of these monthly values, where the weighting was based on the quantity of electricity sold to the community in each month, since this parameter is strongly influenced by seasonal variations in the community’s photovoltaic self-generation capacity. To enhance public acceptance and encourage regional engagement, a 10% discount was applied to the final price offered to the CER as a form of implicit subsidy (GSE, 2023).

For electricity sold to the wholesale market, the same methodology was adopted for price estimation, following identical steps in the analysis of historical and projected PUN zonale data. However, in this case, the 10% discount was not applied, as market sales are not subject to local incentives or community agreements.
