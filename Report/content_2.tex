\subsubsection{Methanation Reactor Modeling Approach}

The methanation reactor was modeled in Modelica to simulate the conversion of hydrogen ($H_{2}$) and carbon dioxide ($CO_{2}$) into methane ($CH_{4}$) and water (H$_{2}O$) via the Sabatier reaction:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CO_2 + 4H_2 \rightarrow CH_4 + 2H_2O} \qquad \Delta H = -165.1\ \text{kJ/mol}
\end{equation}

To approximate the level of $CH_4$ produced from a given feed, a dimensionless parameter $conversionEfficiency$ was introduced. This factor (default: 0.78) reflects the maximum chemical efficiency of catalytic methanation as reported in Götz et al.~\cite{Gotz2016}. It represents the fraction of the limiting reactant (either $CO_{2}$ or $H_{2}$) that is effectively converted to $CH_{4}$, accounting for reactor inefficiencies and incomplete conversion at realistic temperature and pressure conditions.

The molar output flows are then calculated via stoichiometry:
\begin{align*}
\dot{n}_{\mathrm{CH}_4} &= \dot{n}_{\text{lim}} \cdot \eta \\
\dot{n}_{\mathrm{H}_2O} &= 2 \cdot \dot{n}_{\mathrm{CH}_4} \\
\dot{n}_{\mathrm{H}_2} &= \dot{n}_{\mathrm{H}_2,\text{in}} - 4 \cdot \dot{n}_{\mathrm{CH}_4} \\
\dot{n}_{\mathrm{CO}_2} &= \dot{n}_{\mathrm{CO}_2,\text{in}} - \dot{n}_{\mathrm{CH}_4}
\end{align*}
